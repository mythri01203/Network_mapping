# Map Indian plant by evidence, not drawings

FiberGIS should treat every map line as a claim, not a fact: connectivity, length, signal level and permission each need their own record and their own evidence, because geometry proves none of them. For FTTH, the core rule is a per-wavelength, per-direction loss budget with both a ceiling and a floor, since G.984.2 sets 13–28 dB for class B+ and 17–32 dB for C+, which makes too little loss a fault as well as too much. For HFC, the tool needs a directional, frequency-dependent RF tree that can carry AC power and that stores dBµV and dBmV explicitly, yet it must not require DOCSIS, because the available (mostly vendor-listing) evidence suggests Indian LCO plant is often a 1550 nm EDFA feeding low-cost mini-nodes, sharing fibre with GEPON. India adds a layer of its own: pole ownership and permission belong on each pole, because rents vary by state and DISCOMs have repeatedly ordered "unauthorised" cables removed, while the 2024 Right of Way Rules make up-to-date digital records of underground plant a legal duty. Few numbers are universal and no TEC or BSNL values could be retrieved, so nearly every threshold must ship as dated, editable reference data tagged by source. In the field, text-only interfaces failed outright for low-literate first-time users (0 of 20 completed a task, against 20 of 20 on a graphical one), so capture should run on large tiles, camera, automatic GPS, QR scan and photo choices with recorded audio. The engineering arithmetic stays with the system and the office. The usability evidence is old and pre-dates today's smartphones, and every release target in this report is a proposal to test against a pilot, not a finding.

Evidence grades matter here. **Standard** means a standard's text was read (only G.984.2, RFC 7946 and the W3C Geolocation draft reached that bar, and DoT's own slides are the closest thing read for the RoW Rules). **Vendor**, **operator** and **excerpt** values are weaker, and **proposed** marks judgement from this research, which no source states as a rule.

## Five separations stop the map from lying

Bad records are the normal condition, not an edge case. In a 2026 survey, only 13% of operators called their network data largely accurate, though that figure comes from a sponsored survey and is indicative at best ([Fiber Broadband Association](https://fiberbroadband.org/2026/10/08/only-13-of-telecom-operators-say-their-network-data-is-largely-accurate-putting-service-activations-and-ma-at-risk/)). Cable operators describe HFC element and connectivity data as "sometimes inaccurate, out-of-date, or even missing" ([NCTA 2023](https://www.nctatechnicalpapers.com/Paper/2023/3623_Cheng_5154_paper/download)). One Indian ISP's rollout document puts build deviation from design at up to 20% ([Excitel](https://www.slideshare.net/MritunjayKumar114/excitel-fiber-roll-out-doc-17april19)). The schema therefore has to keep apart things that a line on a map blurs together.

| Keep separate | Why it matters | Schema consequence (proposed) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure, cable, connection | A cable between two points does not prove a logical path. Vendor schemas were not public, so this split is inferred from field lists in [FBA](https://fiberbroadband.org/resources/building-better-broadband-accelerating-designs-securing-permit-approvals-chapter-two-fielding-and-base-mapping/) and [Atlas](https://atlas.co/blog/field-app-for-telecom-outside-plant-fiber-and-cell-site-operations/) | Poles and chambers are points, spans and ducts link them, cables run through an ordered list of spans, and splices and patches are explicit records. Express pass-through is distinct from cut and spliced cores |
| Role, construction | Feeder, distribution and drop are roles. ADSS, figure-8 and armoured are builds, and STL's part code separates them ([STL catalogue](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/OFC_Catalogue_Feb20.pdf)) | `role`, `construction`, `installation` and `fibre_type` as separate fields. Never infer role from core count, since a 24F can be one ISP's feeder and another's distribution |
| Core number, colour | "There are many exceptions" to colour codes ([FOA](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ColCodes.htm)), and following fibre numbers is among the most reliable ways to ensure consistent connections ([FOA cross reference](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/install/Color%20Codes%20Cross%20Reference.pdf)) | Identity is tube position, fibre position and absolute number. Colour is derived from `cable.colour_scheme` |
| Drawn, sheath, optical length | The OTDR "measures the length of the fiber, not the cable" ([FOA](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/testing/OTDR/OTDR-FAQS.html)), and metre marks are the best physical measure ([STL](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Fault_Location_Detection_Procedure-_New_Final.pdf)) | `drawn_length_m`, `design_length_m`, `sheath_length_m`, `optical_length_m`, `slack_m`. One never overwrites another |
| Value origin | Taps, splitters and coax report nothing, so their levels are computed or metered. EDFAs report over SNMP, and modems over status pages ([C-COR](https://c-cor.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/C-COR_DS_1550_EYDFA_2.3.pdf); [SpeedGuide](https://speedguide.net/faq/what-cable-modem-signal-levels-are-considered-good-78)) | Every value carries `designed`, `calculated`, `field_measured` or `device_reported`, plus unit, source and date. A calculated value is never shown as measured |

Two organising decisions follow, both proposed. First, split the labour: **the field captures evidence and the system computes engineering**. A worker should never see a loss budget, but the photo, QR scan and meter reading they supply are what make the budget checkable. The small-operator case needs this anyway, because LCO records often lack footage or tap detail, so the tool must run at lower rigour and show lower confidence rather than refuse the data. Second, grade validator severity. Hard errors should cover only physical impossibilities and data-integrity breaks: a recorded splitter loss below the ideal value, or two services on one core. Everything else, including every state rate and every field convention, should be a soft warning with a logged override, so an incorrect rule never blocks work. Each asset should also carry `status` (planned, built, verified) and a confidence basis (OTDR, VFL, power reading, physical inspection, imported), displayed so users stop trusting unverified data.

## PON validity has a floor, a ceiling and two directions

The optics classes set the frame, and they are narrower than vendor marketing suggests. B+ sits in an annex of G.984.2 as "industry best practice" and C+ in an appendix, while the main-body classes are A 5–20, B 10–25 and C 15–30 dB, with a differential path-loss limit of 15 dB ([G.984.2](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.984.2-201908-I!!PDF-E&type=items)). Table A.1's layout was garbled in extraction, but its values agree with G.9805 launch and minimum-loss figures and with the budgets by arithmetic.

| Class | OLT launch (dBm) | OLT Rx sens. / overload | ONU launch | ONU Rx sens. / overload | Path loss (dB) | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| GPON B+ | +1.5 to +5 | −28 / −8 | +0.5 to +5 | −27 / −8 | **13–28** | Standard |
| GPON C+ | +3 to +7 | −32 / −12 (FEC, SOA-type detector) | +0.5 to +5 | −30 / −8 (FEC) | **17–32** | Standard |
| EPON PX20 | +2 to +7 | −27 | −1 to +4 | −24 | 24 channel loss, 1:32 | Vendor, garbled table; column order inferred |
| EPON PX20+ | +2.5 to +7 | −30 | 0 to +4 | −27 | 28 channel loss, 1:64 | Vendor, same caveat |

The GPON rows come from [G.984.2](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.984.2-201908-I!!PDF-E&type=items) and the EPON rows from [FiberMall](https://www.fibermall.com/blog/passive-optical-network-pon.htm). IEEE 802.3 Clause 60 was not accessed, and FOA's EPON table (15–30 and 17–32 dB, 16 splits) disagrees with these figures ([FOA PON](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/appln/FTTH-PON.html)), so the EPON rows stay as editable defaults. XG-PON classes are N1 14–29, N2 16–31, E1 18–33 and E2 20–35 dB, from an excerpt of [G.987.2](https://www.scribd.com/document/685292560/T-REC-G-987-2-202302-I-PDF-E), and XGS-PON uses 1577 nm down and 1270 nm up ([Omada](https://support.omadanetworks.com/in/document/112365/)).

The floor is arithmetic rather than decoration. B+ maximum launch of +5 dBm less 13 dB gives −8 dBm, exactly the overload point, and a C+ ONU at +5 less 17 dB gives −12 dBm, exactly the C+ OLT overload. A validator must therefore flag short, low-split links for too *little* loss, especially C+ optics on a short 1:8 link, and FOA confirms that excess power saturates receivers and that attenuators may be needed ([FOA test](https://foa.org/tech/ref/appln/FTTH-test.html)). Labels cannot be trusted as a shortcut: one "PX20" module shows −30 dBm OLT sensitivity, which matches PX20+ ([Naddod](https://resource.naddod.com/files/%20products-Optical%20Transceivers%20%26%20Cables/2023-09-26/sfp-ep43-px20-datasheet-008213.pdf)), vendors sell "B+" optics claiming 29.5 dB ([Edgeoptic](https://edgeoptic.com/products/xpon/gpon-olt-class-b), excerpt), and C++ at 33 dB does not appear in the G.984.2 tables read ([Omada](https://support.omadanetworks.com/in/document/112365/)). Store the class per PON port, and show the ITU class and the vendor's budget separately.

Reach and split are independent limits that get conflated. G.984.2 gives 20 km maximum fibre distance, 20 km differential reach and 60 km logical reach, with logical reach defined independently of optical budget, and it describes 16, 32 or 64-way splits. Elsewhere, the FTTH Handbook lists 1:128 for B+ ([FTTH Handbook](https://www.plumettaz.com/0/EE8FCFC4B728F572C1258409002593E1/$FILE/FTTH%20Handbook_2017_V8_FINAL.pdf)), FOA lists 64 ([FOA PON](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/appln/FTTH-PON.html)), and a third-party ZTE C320 sheet lists 128 ONUs per port ([ZTE sheet](https://www.telecomate.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ZTE-C320-OLT-Official-Technical-Overview-Datasheet.pdf), excerpt). The resolution is to compute the optical budget from loss and hold `olt_port.max_onu` separately from the vendor. The EPON port limit commonly assumed at 64 could not be confirmed.

### Default losses are assumptions, and the choice changes pass or fail

Use separate values per wavelength and compute upstream and downstream independently, because 1310 nm is the worse case per kilometre and a single dB/km figure hides the limiting direction. Splitter choice alone swings results: FOA's "typical" 19 dB at 1:32 sits 2.5 dB above Efo's 16.5 dB maximum.

The installed unit's datasheet value should override all of these. Worked B+ check at 1310 nm: 5 km × 0.35 + 1:32 at 17 dB + 6 splices at 0.1 + 4 connectors at 0.3 = 20.55 dB, leaving 7.45 dB, or 4.45 dB after a 3 dB margin (Omada recommends 3–5 dB). At 1:64 (20.5 dB) the total is 24.05 dB, leaving 0.95 dB after margin, and stretching that link to 20 km reaches 29.3 dB, which exceeds the budget. Excitel's two-stage 1:8 plus 1:8 at its stated 10.5 dB per stage spends 21 dB on splitters alone ([Excitel](https://www.slideshare.net/MritunjayKumar114/excitel-fiber-roll-out-doc-17april19)). Reserve capacity follows a similar logic: FOA's rule of thumb leaves about 8 of 32 ports open when splitters sit near users ([FOA design](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/appln/FTTH-design.html)).

### ONT receive bands are conventions, so make them settings

No standard defines "good" or "warning". Field sources give −8 to −27 or −28 dBm as the working window ([Huawei MA5833](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1926002/Huawei-Ma5833.html?page=7), excerpt), a consumer guide calls −8 to −22 good and below −27 bad ([SpeedtestHQ](https://speedtesthq.com/guides/diagnostics/read-fiber-light-levels)), and Excitel benchmarks −22 at the ONU ([Excitel](https://www.slideshare.net/MritunjayKumar114/excitel-fiber-roll-out-doc-17april19)). A proposed B+ default is: above −8 "too much light, check for a missing splitter or wrong path"; −8 to −24 good; −24 to −27 "works, little repair margin"; below −27 or LOS bad. New installs take a stricter −22 acceptance, bands are set per class, and the OLT's configured thresholds have the final word. An ONT reading validates only 1490 nm downstream, so the OLT's per-ONT 1310 nm reading must be stored as well. Predicted against measured Rx (OLT launch minus computed loss) is the cheapest whole-path check available: a persistent gap above about 3 dB, a proposed heuristic, points to a dirty connector, a bend, the wrong path or a wrong splitter ratio.

### A valid path is a gapless chain with named ports at both ends

The chain runs OLT PON port → patch → ODF/FMS port → splice → cable core → closures and splices → splitter input → splitter output → cores → terminal port → drop core → ONT. Excitel's design must record the OLT→PON→splitter→port→customer mapping alongside core details and power budget, and it uses S1 PLC 1:8 in a closure then S2 blockless 1:8 in a box, with customers within about 100 m ([Excitel](https://www.slideshare.net/MritunjayKumar114/excitel-fiber-roll-out-doc-17april19)). That single 2019 playbook is not an Indian norm. FOA's cascade examples (2+16, 4+8, 2+4+4) cap the product at 32 ([FOA design](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ref/appln/FTTH-design.html)), so the limit on a path should be configurable. Node names should be functional types (`termination_frame`, `splice_closure`, `splitter_enclosure`, `access_terminal`, `premises`) with operator aliases, because the vocabulary diverges widely: POP/ODF/FDP/FCP/FFP in the Handbook, FDH at FOA, FMS/S1/S2 and no FDT or FAT at Excitel, and FPOI/FTB at BBNL ([BBNL](https://tenders.itiltd.in/assets_admin/dist/img/tendor/41713018NITPIABBNL.pdf)). No Indian standard defining these terms was found.

| Rule (proposed unless cited) | Severity | Catches |
|---|---|---|
| A core or port has one continuation on each side; one occupant per core | Error | Wrong core recorded, ONT light on an "unused" core |
| Two OLT ports may not reach one splitter tree, unless it is a declared dual-input splitter | Error | Unrecorded patches |
| Splitter outputs may not loop back into their own tree | Error | Impossible topology |
| Recorded splitter loss below ideal 10·log₁₀N | Error | Mis-keyed ratio or value |
| Path loss outside the port's class range, in either direction | Warning | Over-budget and overload links |
| Spread on a PON above 15 dB loss or 20 km distance ([G.984.2](https://www.itu.int/rec/dologin_pub.asp?lang=e&id=T-REC-G.984.2-201908-I!!PDF-E&type=items)) | Warning | Differential limit |
| Product of ratios above the configured limit, ONUs above `max_onu` | Warning | Oversubscription |
| Colour mismatch at a splice | Warning only if both cables share a scheme; otherwise compare by number | Replacement cable with a new scheme, a documented repair pattern ([FOA](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/install/Color%20Codes%20Cross%20Reference.pdf)) |
| `tubes × fibres_per_tube ≠ count` | Error | Bad cable definition |
| Mapped path differs from OLT export | Warning | Field patch changes, which the Handbook says become difficult to correct later |
| Splice value recorded in one direction only | Note | MFD differences give a 0.20–0.25 dB directional difference ([FOA](https://FoA.org/tech/ref/install/deliverables.html)) |

### Colour is a lookup that requires a known scheme

TIA-598's order (blue, orange, green, brown, slate, white, red, black, yellow, violet, rose, aqua) dominates ([FOA](https://www.thefoa.org/tech/ColCodes.htm)), and the one Indian operator spec read uses it, printing "pink" for rose ([STL/Bharti](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Bharti-48F-and-96F-NOVA-SSA-Specs2.4mm.pdf)). It is not safe to assume. IEC 60304/DIN-0888 opens with red, green, blue and IEC 60794-2 with blue, yellow, red ([FOA cross reference](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/install/Color%20Codes%20Cross%20Reference.pdf)). An Indian OPGW specification accepts TIA-598, IEC 60304 or GR-20 (excerpt, [Scribd](https://www.scribd.com/document/339798841/Section-02-OPGW)), and the TEC colour table could not be found at all. Within 24-fibre tubes, colours can repeat with only a binder thread separating the groups, or use 24 unique marked colours ([Prysmian](https://na.prysmian.com/sites/na.prysmian.com/files/2025-10/Prysmian%20Color%20Code%20Guide%20for%20Fiber%20Optic%20Specifications.pdf)), so the model also needs a binder or group field. STL's fibres 13–24 carry a black ring and fibre 20 is natural, "customized" by customer ([STL](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Color_Codes_of_Optical_Fiber.pdf)). The number formula is `(tube − 1) × fibres_per_tube + fibre`, so under 12F tubes core 49 is tube 5 (slate), fibre 1 (blue). Do not assume builds: 96F is 8×12F at STL, while FOA shows 24F as 4×6F. Do not auto-generate colours for an unknown scheme. The field message writes itself: "Blue may not be fibre 1. Check the drum."

### Vendor addressing stays verbatim

OLT addressing has 2–5 levels: Huawei `gpon 0/5/0 ont 1` ([Splynx](https://splynx.com/4018/huawei-gpon-configuration/)), ZTE `gpon-onu_1/x/y:n` ([SmartOLT](https://www.smartolt.com/zte-olt-initial-setup.html), excerpt), Nokia `1/1/1/2/33` from a training deck ([GPON deck](https://api.sambuz.com/doc/gpon-basic-configuration-ppt-presentation-901929)), and BDCOM `ePON 0/1:5` ([ixnfo](https://ixnfo.com/?p=15865)). ZTE's SNMP names can even differ by firmware setting ([UserSide](https://wiki.userside.eu/FAQ._ZTE_OLT_equipment_polling)). Hold `olt.vendor`, `address_format`, ordered components, `pon_port_raw` and `onu_id`, and never convert between vendor formats. These come from third-party guides rather than official CLI references, so sample a real device export before finalising import.

## HFC is a powered, directional RF tree, and Indian LCO plant often is not DOCSIS

Classic HFC runs headend or hub → fibre → optical node → trunk coax with amplifiers → feeder with taps and line extenders → drop → STB or modem, with the return path running the opposite way. A node serves about 25 to 2000 homes ([Wikipedia](https://kaweah.freedombox.rocks/kiwix/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2023-10/Hybrid_fiber-coax), excerpt), and coax can carry AC at about 60 or 90 V to power the actives ([NCTA 1995](https://www.nctatechnicalpapers.com/Paper/1995/1995-hybrid-fffier-coax-network-powering-issues/download)). Compared with PON, the tool gains active gain stages, loss that rises with frequency, band-split return paths and a power network, so level calculation is frequency-specific and direction is a property of the path, not of the drawn geometry. A current amplifier shows why these elements must be configurable objects: the Teleste CX3 plugs in its diplex filter (65/85, 85/105, 204/258 MHz) and output module (minimum loss, 2-way, 8 or 14 dB tap), and AC passing is model-dependent ([Teleste CX3](https://www.teleste.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Teleste_Datasheet_CX3_260225.pdf)).

Indian practice is the weakest part of this evidence, so the tool should accommodate it rather than presume it. Regulatory material is solid but does not reach RF plant: TRAI's audit framework concentrates on CAS, SMS, fingerprinting and STB compliance ([TRAI Audit Manual](https://trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-03/Audit_Manual_10032026_0.pdf), excerpt). Engineering evidence is almost entirely vendor listings. A Hyderabad seller lists a 4-port passive node at −15 to +2 dBm input and ≥70 dBµV at −1 dBm, and WDM passive nodes at 45–1000 MHz ([Tejasri](https://m.indiamart.com/tejasrifibernxttechnology/ftth-nodes.html)), plus WDM PON EDFAs at 4–32 outputs ([Tejasri profile](https://m.indiamart.com/tejasrifibernxttechnology/profile.html)). These listings state no return path or modem spec, which suggests one-way RF for TV with data on GEPON. That is an inference from absence, not a survey, and no data was found on the DOCSIS share, RFoG use, analog carriage, LCO coax lengths or operating levels. Older MSO DOCSIS plant may still exist, so CMTS, modem and return path stay optional. Passive and powered "mini-nodes" share one name, which makes `powered: yes/no` mandatory, because the passive unit's budget is far tighter.

Overlay networks force both layers onto one physical record. G/GE-PON uses 1490 nm down and 1310 nm up, 10G PON 1577/1270 nm, and video 1550 nm. A WDM EDFA passes OLT ports at ≤0.8 dB while amplifying video, so PON and video have different budgets through the same box, and connectors differ too: LC/UPC from the OLT, LC/APC toward ONTs ([C-COR EYDFA](https://c-cor.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/C-COR_DS_1550_EYDFA_2.3.pdf)). Model a fibre span once with a wavelength list and run two calculations over it: the PON budget in dBm, and the RF overlay from EDFA port to receiver in dBm and then dBµV from receiver onward. A vendor claims PLC splitters pass 1310/1490/1550 nm without separate stages ([TTI Fiber](https://www.ttifiber.com/products/fiber-optic-splitter/), excerpt), but per-wavelength splitter loss and a typical 1550 nm overlay budget were not confirmed, so include a per-wavelength loss field. RFoG sits between the layers at 1550 nm down and 1610 nm up on vendor sheets ([CommScope](https://fr.commscope.com/globalassets/digizuite/61667-cp85x7u-8u.pdf), excerpt), with a beat-interference risk when R-ONUs transmit together, and those sheets should outrank a mirror article describing 1490/1310 nm.

### Levels are per device, and only the unit conversion is a constant

**dBµV = dBmV + 60** is the one hard constant ([SpeedGuide](https://speedguide.net/faq/what-cable-modem-signal-levels-are-considered-good-78)). At 75 Ω, dBµV = dBm + 108.75 (calculated from P = V²/75). Indian meters and datasheets tend to use dBµV and DOCSIS literature uses dBmV, so a 60 dB offset is the likeliest entry error: store the unit with each value and display both. Thresholds belong in switchable profiles (Indian TV STB, DOCSIS 3.0, DOCSIS 3.1). No India-specific level plan, typical amplifier operating level, tilt recommendation or tap-versus-distance table was found, and Umax figures such as the CX3's 110 dBµV are ratings, not targets, so store each node's designed levels instead of assuming one.

Two illustrative budgets show what recording these values buys. A passive node at ≥70 dBµV against a 57 dBµV minimum leaves about 13 dB, which is one 4-way splitter plus a short drop, and that margin shrinks quickly as optical input falls, so optical input should be recorded for every passive node. An 80 dBµV receiver through an 8-way (11.5 dB) and 30 m of RG6 at 750 MHz (about 5.5 dB) lands near 63 dBµV, inside the 57–77 window. Both rest on dated or listing-grade figures. Treat them as a test of the calculator, not as design guidance.

### Connection rules are directional, and termination rules remain unsourced

These rules derive from datasheets and are proposed validators rather than a standard, since no SCTE operational practice on tap and amp connection was retrieved. The Cisco tap datasheet separates through loss from tap value ([Cisco](https://www3-realm.cisco.com/en/US/prod/collateral/video/ps8806/ps9075/ps9091/product_data_sheet0900aecd806c7a16.pdf), excerpt), so mixing them is a modelling error, and taps typically block AC toward the subscriber ([US 5909154](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/5909154), excerpt).

| Rule | Severity | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Optical port connects to RF only through a node, receiver or R-ONU | Error | Element definition |
| 1310 nm transmitter into an EDFA | Error | EDFA band 1545–1565 nm ([C-COR](https://c-cor.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/C-COR_DS_1550_EYDFA_2.3.pdf)) |
| PON wavelengths through a plain CATV EDFA | Error | Standard CATV EDFAs amplify only 1550 nm, and xPON needs a passive pass port ([Premlink](https://www.premlink.net/choosing-a-wdm-edfa-guide/), excerpt) |
| APC to UPC mismatch | Warning | [C-COR](https://c-cor.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/C-COR_DS_1550_EYDFA_2.3.pdf) port types |
| Amp input must face upstream and outputs downstream, along the path | Error | [Teleste](https://www.teleste.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Teleste_Datasheet_CX3_260225.pdf) block diagram |
| Tap IN and OUT stay on the line; drop ports feed only drop, ground block or subscriber splitter | Warning | Inferred from tap construction |
| Power calculated only along AC-passing elements; warn at device minimum voltage and on inserter-leg over-current (one 60 VAC case had almost 11 A on a leg) | Warning | [Bowers](https://broadbandlibrary.com/basic-hfc-ac-design-part-one-2/) |
| Amplifier cascade above a configured limit | Warning | No standard limit found: a dated paper gives two trunks, a bridger and three extenders ([NCTA 1996](https://www.nctatechnicalpapers.com/Paper/1996/1996-ingress-margin-improvement-in-sub-low-return-hfc-plant/download)), and a glossary says 3–5 ([RF Essentials](https://rfessentials.com/resources/rf-glossary/cable-amplifier/)) |
| Total OFDM power used to set levels | Warning | [VIAVI](https://www.normann-engineering.com/support/presentations/{8db11f80-96de-453e-bdb5-d060db6bae9e}_VIAVI_DOCSISPoster_v9_FINAL.pdf) says to use the 6 MHz-referenced level |

Feeder-end termination and 75 Ω terminators on unused tap ports are standard trade lore that this research could not source, so ship those as soft prompts and have an RF engineer review them before they harden.

Measurement design follows the same evidence logic. The CX3 has 20 dB test points, so a reading must record `test_point_loss_db` or the tool under-reports by 20 dB. EDFAs report over LCD and SNMP. Handheld meters read level, MER, BER and tilt ([JDSU DSAM](https://www.electronicsdatasheets.com/download/54da101be34e2421522db7da.pdf?format=pdf), excerpt), and the useful comparison is across tap, ground block and outlet, since a change between them isolates drop or home wiring faults ([VIAVI](https://www.normann-engineering.com/support/presentations/{8db11f80-96de-453e-bdb5-d060db6bae9e}_VIAVI_DOCSISPoster_v9_FINAL.pdf)). A measurement record should hold port, timestamp, instrument, frequency, level plus unit, MER, BER, tilt, technician and origin. Listed Indian passive nodes mention no management, so they probably expose nothing.

Dedicated coax tools give a useful template: Bentley Coax routes graphically on poles, pedestals, manholes and duct with real-time signal and tap sizing ([Bentley](https://es-la.bentley.com/pds-bentley-coax-ltr-en-lr), excerpt), and Schneider places nodes and taps on strand points from catalogue defaults, with amplifier profiles ([Schneider](https://www.productinfo.schneider-electric.com/arcfmsolution/5b9145ca62e672000167792a/Designer%20Hybrid%20Fiber%20Coax/English/Designer%20Hybrid%20Fiber%20Coax%20(bookmap)_0000232414.xml/$/PlaceRFNetwork_30), excerpt). No coax model surfaced for VETRO, or explicit HFC types for 3-GIS, though absence from search is not proof ([Lightwave](https://www.lightwaveonline.com/fttx/ftth-b/article/16654784/vetro-fibermap-offers-cloud-based-fttx-network-mapping); [3-GIS](https://www.3-gis.com/telecom/telecom-asset-inventory-management)). If that gap is real, combined PON and coax overlay modelling is a differentiator worth validating.

## A phone gives a 5 m guess, so position and length need provenance

Plan on about 5 m at best. GPS phones reach about 4.9 m under open sky and worse near buildings and trees ([gps.gov](https://www.gps.gov/node/70), excerpt). The browser number needs care: W3C now specifies `accuracy` as a 68% radius ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)), Android agrees ([Android](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/Location?hl=cs), excerpt), and MDN still says 95% ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/GeolocationCoordinates/accuracy), excerpt). Follow W3C. For a circular Gaussian error, the 95% radius is about 1.6 times the 68% radius, so "5 m" means about 8 m at 95%, and street-canyon multipath can break even that. `enableHighAccuracy` is only a hint, and non-secure contexts fail, so HTTPS is required ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/geolocation/)). ODK's defaults show the field norm, auto-capturing at 5 m, warning only at 100 m without enforcing, and storing accuracy 0 on map-placed points ([ODK](https://docs.getodk.org/form-question-types/)). The 100 m warning is far too lax for assets metres apart, and ODK's zero is a warning about how placed points can look perfect. QField offers a useful pattern in sample averaging and a red, yellow and green requirement that blocks red vertices ([QField](https://docs.qfield.org/how-to/navigation-and-positioning/gnss/)).

Proposed thresholds, from no telecom standard: for poles, closures and manholes, accept at ≤5 m, warn at 5–10 m and above 10 m require a map-placed correction with a reason. For route vertices, accept up to about 15 m, because poles anchor the route. Store method (GPS, GPS-averaged, map-placed, imported), reported accuracy, sample count and timestamp per point, so these limits can change later. Averaging is worth doing cheaply but mainly removes jitter, since multipath errors correlate over short periods, and no controlled phone test of it was found. Do not auto-shift to imagery: Google Earth in India measured 3.75–6.83 m RMSE against WorldView with no apparent systematic error ([ISPRS 2020](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLIII-B3-2020/1333/2020/isprs-archives-XLIII-B3-2020-1333-2020.pdf)), so a fixed correction has no support and imagery is no more absolute than the phone. No study of ordinary phone accuracy in Indian cities was found, so FiberGIS's own captures should become that data.

### Map length under-reports, raw tracks over-report, and sag is not the reason

Sag is nearly irrelevant. FOA limits it to under 2% of span ([FOA](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/OSP_Construction/Aerial_Workmanship.html)), and a shallow parabola at 2% adds only about 0.11%, roughly 1 m per km, so any "3–5% sag factor" is covering other things. The real gap is slack loops, vertical runs on poles and into chambers, corners drawn straight, tails in closures, and undocumented detours, and even small route errors "can cause havoc during precise fault location" ([STL](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Fault_Location_Detection_Procedure-_New_Final.pdf)). Raw walking tracks err the opposite way: four devices on one hike differed by roughly 13% and one track ran 1,307 ft against a traced 1,010 ft, though in a hiking context ([CalTopo](https://blog.caltopo.com/2020/10/12/2232/)). Use tracks to place vertices, and never for length.

The expected relationships are `sheath ≈ drawn + slack + vertical + tails` and `optical ≈ sheath × (1 + excess)`, with excess at 1–2% ([FOA](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/testing/OTDR/OTDR-FAQS.html)), 2–3% on stranded loose tube ([Corning](https://www.corning.com/catalog/coc/documents/application-engineering-notes/AEN142.pdf), excerpt), and up to about 1.04 ([Borzycki](https://yadda.icm.edu.pl/baztech/element/bwmeta1.element.baztech-a180ee63-5e05-4dd9-ad69-2fa1502e965d/c/JTiT_2021_4_Borzycki.pdf), excerpt). Marks are typically ±0.5% accurate from the factory, and fault location uses a correction of sheath distance over measured fibre length ([STL](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Fault_Location_Detection_Procedure-_New_Final.pdf)). Proposed flags, with no standard behind them: optical shorter than sheath by over about 1% suggests a wrong core, wrong cable or an unrecorded break; optical longer by over about 5% suggests unrecorded slack or a detour; map against installed length differing by over 10% needs review; and a long aerial route with no slack entries means slack was not captured. Marks should be photographed at both ends and either side of each loop, with the unit noted, since some cable is marked in feet. Replaced cable makes marks jump, and marks can count down as well as up, so use the absolute difference. OTDR distance alone does not prove identity, so VFL or light levels confirm which fibre.

Slack needs recording as point assets, and its defaults must not be presented as rules. Indian aerial instructions repeat about 10 m at a splice location ([BBNL](https://tenders.itiltd.in/assets_admin/dist/img/tendor/41713018NITPIABBNL.pdf); [OPTCL](https://www.optcl.co.in/writereaddata/Tender/212218042227SectionVIIEngineeringInstruction_07032018.pdf)). The Handbook gives about 20 m at chambers and 3–5 m per joint as a route allowance ([FTTH Handbook](https://www.plumettaz.com/0/EE8FCFC4B728F572C1258409002593E1/$FILE/FTTH%20Handbook_2017_V8_FINAL.pdf)). One Army network spec demands at least 40 m in pull-through chambers ([ITI](https://tenders.itiltd.in/assets_admin/dist/img/tendor/35675789piaofcnit.pdf)), and **no source confirmed any "X metres per km" rule, including the oft-repeated 15 m/km**. The loss impact is tiny (20 m at 0.35 dB/km is 0.007 dB), but each uncounted loop can shift a fault by its full length. Bend limits go with the cable: STL's 96F specifies 20D, so 250 mm at 12.5 mm diameter ([STL/Bharti](https://stl.tech/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Bharti-48F-and-96F-NOVA-SSA-Specs2.4mm.pdf)). Loops belong on a fixture at the pole end of a span, never mid-span ([FOA](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/OSP_Construction/Aerial_Workmanship.html)). BBNL's 2 km typical ADSS joint spacing gives a plausibility check, not a rule.

OTDR results also need context. Test from the subscriber toward the CO, because from the OLT side the backscatter from all branches adds together, and test points around splitters help with cascades ([FOA test](https://foa.org/tech/ref/appln/FTTH-test.html)). In-service testing uses filtered 1625 or 1650 nm ([EXFO](https://www.exfo.com/contentassets/d407e533bf304cf8a8d635f389a1bc3a/exfo_anote201_otdr-pon-testing_en.pdf)). Save traces against the asset. BBNL requires PON OTDR readings at OLT, splitter, joint and ONT, with coil length on each side, and its as-builts use 200–250 m numbered grid sheets recording depth, spare fibres and three-point chamber references ([BBNL](https://tenders.itiltd.in/assets_admin/dist/img/tendor/41713018NITPIABBNL.pdf)), which sets a template for government-linked exports.

### Keep the required record short and let the office fill the rest

Over-collection adds schedule and cost, and owners' differing requirements force re-collection if not checked first ([FBA](https://fiberbroadband.org/resources/building-better-broadband-accelerating-designs-securing-permit-approvals-chapter-two-fielding-and-base-mapping/)). The minimum record below is proposed, built from FBA, Atlas and FOA field lists, and everything outside it is an office add-on.

| Asset | Required in the field | Add later |
|---|---|---|
| Pole | ID, location with accuracy and source, owner, material, photo | Power on pole (LT, 11 kV, none), condition, attachments, permission reference |
| Aerial span | From and to pole, cable ID, mapped length | Measured length, road crossing, clearance |
| Cable | ID, core count, owner | Type, marks, install date, colour scheme |
| Slack loop | Host, length, cable ID | Mark difference as proof |
| Duct | Route, count and diameter | Depth seen, occupancy, method, permit depth |
| Manhole or handhole | ID, location, type | Cover and inside photos, ducts, slack, water |
| Closure | ID, location and host, type and capacity | Cables in and out, splices, test results |
| FAT or splitter | ID, parent, ratio, photo | Ports used and free |
| Building | Name or address, location | Entry, riser, permission, drop source |
| Permission | Authority, type, reference, dates and expiry | Route covered, fee, document scan |

Identity should use two values: an immutable short code on a QR tag plus a display name such as `KRM-FAT-0142`, with no parent encoded because parents change on re-splice. A DISCOM's number goes in `owner_pole_no`, never in the key, since it may be absent, repeated or repainted. No Indian naming convention or DoT/TEC labelling rule was found, and the claim that tags reduce duplicates comes from a tag vendor ([Camcode](https://camcode.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Partner-With-Camcode-Sell-Sheet.pdf), excerpt). Duplicates are mostly a workflow problem, so before creating a point, list same-type assets within about 1.5 times the GPS radius plus a margin and ask "same as this one?" with its photo. A scan opens the existing record. Spans link existing poles rather than free lines, and merges keep the older ID with a pointer.

Interchange follows simple rules. GeoJSON and KML both run longitude first on WGS 84, and six decimals is about 10 cm ([RFC 7946](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7946)). Within India, latitude (about 6–38°N) and longitude (68–98°E) do not overlap, so an importer can detect swaps automatically. DoT's GatiShakti material reportedly asks IP-I holders for KML or shapefile with operator, backhaul source, install date and core counts ([C-DOT](https://dotws.cdot.in/sites/default/files/PM%20GatiShakti%20Nation%20Master%20Plan%20Mapping%20of%20optical%20fiber%20by%20IP-I%20registration%20holders.pdf?download=1), excerpt), ITI requires KMZ/KML route maps ([ITI](https://tenders.itiltd.in/assets_admin/dist/img/tendor/35675789piaofcnit.pdf)), and Excitel surveys in KML and designs in SHP ([Excitel](https://www.slideshare.net/MritunjayKumar114/excitel-fiber-roll-out-doc-17april19)). On import, accept KMZ and folders, keep `name` and free-text `description` verbatim, ask rather than assume that a Placemark is a pole or a line is one cable, drop usually-zero altitude, and never trust KML length. Calculate length geodesically, because Web Mercator inflates by 1/cos(latitude): about 1.01 at 8°N, 1.15 at 30°N and 1.22 at 35°N, a bias that grows northward across India.

## India makes every pole a permission record and a safety question

The central framework is clear and generous, but it does not settle the real dispute. The Telecommunications (Right of Way) Rules, 2024 took effect on 1 January 2025. DoT's slides set ₹1,000/km one-time application fees for underground and overground, NIL for establishing a pole, ₹100 per street-furniture item per year for a telecom line, and deemed permission with no decision in 67 days, and they bar public entities from levying other charges ([DoT slides](https://anumati.wb.gov.in/assets/pdf/PPT-on-The-Telecommunications-Act-2023-RoW-Rules-2024.pdf)). JSA's summary reads 45 days as deemed approval ([JSA](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/media-telecoms-it-entertainment/telecommunication-right-of-way-rules-2024-jsa)), while DoT's slides treat 45 as the intent-to-reject point. DoT deserves more weight, but gazette GSR 576(E) was not read and should decide it.

Three rule items should shape the schema directly. Rule 13 requires "up-to-date digital information of underground telecommunication network... including as-built information", shared on demand through the portal. Rule 19 gives a provider 24 hours in an emergency, or 7 days otherwise, to supply network details to people about to dig. Rule 18 gives 30 days to plan and 90 days to complete a removal on the owner's notice ([DoT slides](https://anumati.wb.gov.in/assets/pdf/PPT-on-The-Telecommunications-Act-2023-RoW-Rules-2024.pdf)). Duct route, depth, count and as-built geometry are therefore first-class data, and the tool should produce a "what lies here" export quickly. The wording covers underground networks, so an equivalent aerial duty should not be claimed. The draft's lat/long and route-plan requirements for overground applications were not confirmed in the final text ([Draft](https://taxguru.in/?p=923602)).

Electric poles are where small ISPs and LCOs face conflict. DoT's 2022 table shows annual rents from ₹50 rural Odisha to ₹1,500 in Assam, with Delhi at ₹20,000 per pole for 5 years ([NBM](https://eservices.dot.gov.in/sites/default/files/circular-notifications/NBM_presentation_with_Discom_12-Aug-22__Final.pdf)), and KSEB charges ₹300 urban and ₹145 panchayat with a 3% annual rise ([KSEB](https://ctvtest.kseb.in/bo/2021/5.b.o._rates_wef_514_2021%20dtd%2001.04.2021dt_05.07.2021.pdf), excerpt). So an accurate pole count is money: 1,000 poles at ₹300 is ₹3 lakh a year. BESCOM's removal notices followed a pole collapse that injured a student, and it set a July 2024 deadline with providers held liable ([News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/internet-services-bengaluru-could-get-affected-bescom-orders-illegal-cable-removal-181534); [Web Josh](https://webjosh.com/article/the-hindu-impact-bescom-sets-july-8-deadline-for-removal-of-illegal-cables-laid-on-its-infrastructure%C2%A0in-bengaluru-1720262164-4936), excerpts). Operators claimed they had paid and held permission while BBMP said it gives none ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/bengaluru/bescom-deadline-to-isps-triggers-internet-disruption-impacts-work-from-home-employees-2660088), excerpt), and that dispute is what per-pole records should address. Geotagged, dated photos with a permission reference could help an operator when a cutting drive arrives, though that benefit is inference and not a sourced outcome. Whether the ₹100 rate overrides state DISCOM rates, whether private DISCOMs are bound (not yet notified as public entities in that deck), and whether MIB-registered LCOs count as "authorised" all need legal confirmation, so the app must present neither rate as the rule.

Depth is the clearest trap for a hard-coded value. A figure of 1.65 m depth and 0.45 m width repeats in NH permission files ([MoRTH](https://morth.gov.in/sites/default/files/45410-01052022111017(1)(1).pdf), excerpt), but no national DoT, TEC or BSNL norm fixing it was found, and a 2010 circular points to depth being set in each permission ([MoRTH 2010](https://morth.gov.in/sites/default/files/circulars_document/US-2010.09.21-Use%20of%20NH%20land%20for%20laying%20of%20Utilities%20including%20levying%20of%20appropriate%20fees.pdf), excerpt). Store observed depth beside permit-specified depth and compare those two. As-built depth matters in its own right, because design-recorded depth can be nearly a metre out in one reported example ([EditFast](https://editfast.com/editing-services/telecommunications-and-infrastructure/fibre-and-network-infrastructure/)).

Safety guidance must be short, clear and reviewed. FOA's core rule is that telecom cable stays in the telecom space, below power, with clearance held at mid-span where sag reduces it ([FOA](https://www.foa.org/tech/ref/OSP_Construction/Aerial_Workmanship.html)). The only Indian numbers found are a reported 2004 Karnataka circular: 3.05 m above ground along roads, 5.8 m across roads, 1.2 m below LT, 1.8 m below 11 kV, and tapping at least 2 m away from poles ([Indian Television](https://indiantelevision.com/?p=191809)). Those are over 20 years old, and the CEA 2023 safety regulations ([CEA](https://kseb.in/uploads/Downloadtemsuppy/Central%20Electricity%20Authority%20(Measures%20relating%20to%20Safety%20and%20Electric%20Supply)%20Regulations,%202023-1699594973944568556.pdf)) were not read, so present clearances as state-and-date reference data pointing to current DISCOM conditions. Proposed app copy, which needs a qualified safety reviewer before shipping because no Indian field-safety guide was found: mapping never requires climbing or touching a pole; treat every wire, stay and metal pole as live; do not survey poles in rain or lightning; do not raise rods or ladders near conductors; report leaning, sparking or burning poles to the DISCOM; do not stand in traffic for a photo; do not use the phone while riding. "Hazard seen" should be a one-tap report that never rewards moving closer for a better photo or GPS fix.

## Field capture works by removing reading, typing and menus

The closest evidence comes from Bangalore. Among 58 low-literate first-time users on a mobile banking task, **text UI completion was 0/20, voice 13/18 and graphical 20/20**, though graphical took 13 minutes and about 14 prompts because people feared they would "spoil" the phone ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). Hierarchy fails independently of text: 56 of 90 subjects across four countries could not initially navigate menus, 23 of the 34 who managed on their own handset failed on another, and 48 did not grasp vertical scrollbars ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). Literacy and Raven's scores predicted hierarchy performance even with no text on screen ([CHI 2013](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=164235), abstract). In a small study of older UK adults, a flat icon grid had the highest task success despite the most errors, because people could recover ([Skarlatidou 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10078615/1/ecscw2019_Skarlatidou_ep02.pdf)).

Audiences differ, and the default should serve most of them. In the closest regional study, Kannada semiliterate users cut task time 44% and errors 62% within 1.5 hours, most preferred sound plus text, and they wanted audio kept for confidence even as use fell. The authors conclude that adding audio to text is easier than building a text-free UI ([Findlater 2009](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/public_user/ravin/papers/chi2009_literacy.pdf)). So the proposed design is one interface with text, icon and an always-available speaker button, with every core task completable without reading, and no separate "illiterate mode" that users may refuse and that doubles test burden. These studies used feature phones and PDAs from 2007–2013, and whether today's WhatsApp and YouTube users still show these barriers is unmeasured, which could change the priorities.

The evidence on errors reshapes what validation can achieve. Trained, literate workers in Gujarat on ordinary forms erred on 4.2% of entries (7.6% for health workers), with an estimated 38% of forms containing an error, and 12 of 26 errors were dangerous, of which only one was detectable from the data ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). **Wrong selections pass validation because each looks valid.** So photos are not decoration. They are the only audit path for choice errors, and spot audits by a person remain necessary. Barcode-style entry reached 1% or below after 5–15 minutes' comfort time ([CAM via Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)), and an NFC tag-and-map approach reached 97.5% task success in the Congo Basin ([Skarlatidou 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10078615/1/ecscw2019_Skarlatidou_ep02.pdf)). Neither tested weathered labels. No study has compared one-question-per-screen with multi-field forms, so the wizard below is a defensible default rather than a proven one.

### The capture flow is six steps and about five taps

The proposed pattern for a pole, box or drop runs as follows. The worker taps a large spoken tile such as "Add box". The camera opens first, with a framing overlay and a short spoken instruction. GPS is captured automatically with a progress state and no coordinates shown. The worker scans the QR on a pre-printed tag where one exists. One or two photo-grid choices auto-advance, using ODK-style image grids and quick advance ([ODK](https://docs.getodk.org/form-question-types/)). A summary card shows photo, icon and place, reads aloud, and offers a large Save and a "fix" control. Linking replaces drawing: pick a nearby existing pole, tap "next pole", take one photo, and the span auto-fills.

Numeric entry deserves its own design, because it is where this domain and this audience collide. Half of observed errors fell in the 3 numeric fields out of 12 questions, including backspace trouble and decimals such as "1006" for 100.6, and users could not find `*` or `#` ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). Yet PON acceptance depends on values like −21.5 dBm, and Excitel's own document omits the sign ([Excitel](https://www.slideshare.net/MritunjayKumar114/excitel-fiber-roll-out-doc-17april19)). The proposal is that **the worker never types a minus sign, a decimal point or a unit**. A dBm pad supplies the sign and fixed decimal, the value is read back aloud, and a photo of the meter display or cable mark is captured as proof, so the office can correct it. Range checks then catch the overlooked cases, since over a third of errors were detectable from data alone. The RF equivalent is a pad locked to the meter's unit, which removes the 60 dB error. Do not ask workers to read 24-hour clocks or work out time spans ([Blue Ventures](https://blueventures.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Using-ODK-Guide_Setting-up-mobile-monitoring-1.3-August-2017.pdf), excerpt).

The map should not depend on being read. Bangalore residents relied on landmarks rather than compass directions or street names, and none could locate important landmarks on a commercial text-based map, though the formal sample was tiny ([Medhi 2007](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mas-965-nextlab-i-designing-mobile-technologies-for-the-next-billion-users-fall-2008/8ccecb3eacd514b7ea0255f5158de907_MITMAS_965F08_medhi2007.pdf)). Streets in India are often unnamed or multiply named ([Laraki](https://elizlaraki.substack.com/p/google-maps-ux-the-india-conundrum)). So the app opens centred on GPS with a large "you are here" marker, shows a distance and an arrow to the target, and labels with temple, school, petrol bunk and photos from earlier captures. Workers add voice notes such as "near the temple" and landmarks grow as capture proceeds. Capture is "I am at this pole", with drag only as a reviewed correction. **No study tested how low-literate Indian users read satellite imagery**, and people prefer realistic displays even when these hurt performance ([ICA 2018](https://ica-proc.copernicus.org/articles/1/20/2018/ica-proc-1-20-2018.pdf)), so A/B test satellite against a simple base with a one-tap toggle. Literal reading has real consequences for styling: arrows for traffic failed until replaced by cars, yellow roads were rejected because "roads can never be yellow", and a house icon read as a village hut ([Medhi 2007](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mas-965-nextlab-i-designing-mobile-technologies-for-the-next-billion-users-fall-2008/8ccecb3eacd514b7ea0255f5158de907_MITMAS_965F08_medhi2007.pdf)). Use photos of this operator's actual hardware for specific equipment, cartoons with action cues for verbs, plausible colours and no legends.

### Audio must be recorded, and language must be collected from crews

Do not rely on `speechSynthesis`. Chrome on Android does not return actual installed voices and "will default to an English voice" when a pack is missing ([Readium](https://readium.org/speech/docs/WebSpeech.html)), the voice list can be empty on first call ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/SpeechSynthesis/getVoices)), and Android WebView does not support the API at all ([Chromium](https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/XpkevOngqUs)). Ship human-recorded clips of fixed prompts, cached offline, in Kannada, Telugu, Hindi and English, validated with field staff ([Dimagi](https://dimagi.atlassian.net/wiki/x/-jPKfw), excerpt). Splice pre-recorded digits for dynamic numbers, or pre-generate with cloud TTS and human review, and use a native TTS bridge in any WebView wrapper. Telugu read by an English voice is worse than silence, so show the speaker only where a recording exists. Scanning has the same trap: `BarcodeDetector` is experimental with limited availability ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/BarcodeDetector)), so bundle a JavaScript or WASM scanner. Offline maps must be packaged, since online basemaps show only when connected ([ODK](https://docs.getodk.org/collect-offline-maps/)).

For wording, literal translation fails ([ODK](https://docs.getodk.org/guide-form-language/)), and translation cannot fix an unfamiliar concept ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). Evidence on loanwords conflicts and no controlled Telugu or Kannada study exists, so build the glossary by recording crew conversations and running a recognition test by region. Author-suggested candidates such as ಕಂಬ, స్తంభం and खंभा for "pole" against their loanwords are hypotheses only, and Telangana wording should not be transferred to coastal Andhra without checking. Write loanwords in local script, because users may understand a spoken word but not its Roman spelling (excerpt, [Think with Google](https://www.thinkwithgoogle.com/intl/en-apac/future-of-marketing/creativity/app-website-localization-indian-users)), put a photo beside each term, and offer English freely, since non-fluent users often choose it for identity reasons ([Karusala 2018](https://redi.cedia.edu.ec/document/1644), abstract). Engineering practice: store canonical codes rather than strings, use ICU message keys with no concatenated sentences, and use Western digits unless a region proves otherwise.

### Sizes, script and failure states

Platform minimums are floors: WCAG 2.2 sets 24 CSS px and 44 at the enhanced level ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)), and Android says 48dp, "larger is even better" ([Android](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/apps)). MIL-STD figures reported at 15 mm bare and 20 mm gloved are unchecked against the standard ([study record](https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsair,edsair.doi...........9465c900e5780072de7a168d106fd14d), excerpt). No study sets a number for this audience, so proposed values are 48dp with 8dp spacing as the absolute floor, 64–72dp for routine controls, full-width primary buttons at least 72dp tall in the bottom third, and an optional 2-column mode at about 126dp (20 mm) for gloves. Essential tasks avoid long-press, drag and pinch, since tapping and long-press confusion and rough skin are observed problems ([Skarlatidou 2019](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10078615/1/ecscw2019_Skarlatidou_ep02.pdf)). For contrast, use 7:1 for text and 4.5:1 for icons as a margin, knowing WCAG did not address glare ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-enhanced.html)), and never encode meaning in colour alone. No rigorous light-versus-dark study exists for phones in sun, so test on low-cost LCDs at midday.

UX4G specifies Noto Sans, at least 16px body, and line height 1.8 for Devanagari ([UX4G](https://www.ux4g.gov.in/foundations/typography)), and Material lists Telugu among tall scripts ([Material](https://m2.material.io/design/typography/language-support.html), excerpt). Starting values to test, not validated figures, are Kannada and Telugu body at 18sp, weight 500, line height 1.7–1.8. Self-host subsetted [Noto Sans Telugu](https://fontsource.org/fonts/noto-sans-telugu/about) and Kannada for offline use, set `lang`, use `rem`, never fix button heights, and check conjuncts at the largest system scale.

Fear of breaking the phone is a documented barrier, but no study tested undo or error wording with this audience, so these are reasoned designs to test. Make every field action undoable and say so aloud, keep server-side history, and reserve confirmation for destructive or external actions, with the item's photo and outcome-labelled buttons ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)). Errors become next actions with an image ("Go to open sky. Wait for the green tick."), with the code logged rather than shown, and consequences stated plainly ("Customer's line will show on the wrong pole"). Sync has three fixed states with icon and spoken phrase: "Saved on phone", "Sending" and "Sent to office", with counts in digits. Save every step locally and submit idempotently, because anxious users may repeat an entry. Give sound, vibration and a tick after each save. Autosave silently rather than offering a "save incomplete" choice, which caused confusion and lost work at Dimagi ([Dimagi](https://dimagi.atlassian.net/wiki/x/-jPKfw), excerpt). A one-tap supervisor call or voice note serves as an escape for odd cases: a live operator was about nine times more accurate than forms (0.45% against 4.2%) ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)), so workers should never be pushed into guessing.

## Prove it in the street, because preference data will lie

Training dose matters: novices reached under 2% error with an hour of PDA training against 14% with 2–3 minutes, human mediation "was critical for most successful transactions", and manuals went mostly unread ([Medhi 2011](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/ToCHI2711_Medhi.pdf)). Women in Chhattisgarh learned specific tasks in 45-minute sessions ([Yale](https://ie.yale.edu/gender-and-growth-gaps-project/policy-briefs/accelerating-indian-womens-use-mobile-phones-through-low-cost-training-digital-skills-improves-their), excerpt), and groups behaved more boldly than individuals ([Medhi 2007](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mas-965-nextlab-i-designing-mobile-technologies-for-the-next-billion-users-fall-2008/8ccecb3eacd514b7ea0255f5158de907_MITMAS_965F08_medhi2007.pdf)). The proposed rollout is a 45–60 minute session in groups of 3–5 on workers' own phones, a visibly marked practice workspace, offline videos of 60–90 seconds per task showing local crews ([Ramachandran 2010](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=159425) supports short phone video, abstract), and a rotated buddy period with the solo rate measured after, since a buddy can mask problems by taking the phone. Sign-off is two consecutive perfect practice captures, while remembering that the same threshold still produced 7.6% errors later. Feedback runs weekly as photo or voice-note coaching, never punishment, which would teach workers to hide errors. Demo mode and buddying have no direct evidence and should be measured.

Testing has to route around a measured bias: in 450 Bangalore interviews, respondents were about 2.5 times more likely to prefer the technology they believed the interviewer had built, about 5 times with a foreign interviewer using a translator, and this held even with a deliberately degraded artefact ([Dell 2012](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=162695), abstract). Use a same-language moderator not presented as the builder, give tasks by voice or picture card against a physical target, embed them in stories ("a friend needs..."), since one participant grew agitated at direct framing ([Medhi 2007](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/mas-965-nextlab-i-designing-mobile-technologies-for-the-next-billion-users-fall-2008/8ccecb3eacd514b7ea0255f5158de907_MITMAS_965F08_medhi2007.pdf)), and pay at least a casual day's wage ([Findlater 2009](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/public_user/ravin/papers/chi2009_literacy.pdf)). Log prompts at levels 0–3, where needing "show me how" counts as failure, and compare against the current process, whether paper or WhatsApp photos.

No benchmark exists for this kind of capture. These proposed starting targets for "capture one network asset" should be recalibrated from a baseline pilot and judged by physical audit against independently recorded ground truth.

| Metric | First unaided attempt after training | After one week |
|---|---|---|
| Completion without "show me how" | ≥70% | ≥90% |
| Median time per asset, excluding walking | <3 min | <90 s |
| Taps, common case | ≤8 | ≤8 |
| Record-level error by physical audit | ≤10% | ≤5% |
| Critical error (wrong type, or location beyond tolerance) | ≤3% | ≤1% |
| Usable audit photo | ≥90% | ≥95% |
| Lost data | 0 | 0 |
| Completion gap, lowest to highest literacy group | ≤15 points | ≤10 points |

Run rounds of 12–16 workers mixed by state and literacy (a private read-aloud screen, including women if crews do), on real streets at midday, with a dead-zone segment and noise. Tasks cover adding a tagged pole, adding an untagged box, a cable run, finding an asset, walking to a target, undo, recovering from no GPS, and syncing. Report counts with intervals, because 10/12 is about 54–96% on an adjusted Wald interval and is good only for finding major problems. Follow with a pilot of 30–50 workers and several hundred captures, and audit 5–10% of assets at 1 and 4 weeks. Check comprehension by asking people to explain "Saved on phone" in their own words, not by SUS, for which no validated Kannada or Telugu version was found. Any critical-error pattern or lost-data event blocks release regardless of averages. Any problem seen in three or more participants is fixed before the next round.

## Conclusion

The engineering and the usability evidence point to the same architecture from opposite directions. Rule-based validators cannot catch a wrong choice that looks valid, and low-literate workers cannot be asked to carry dB arithmetic, so the system's real asset is cheap field evidence (QR scans, geotagged photos, photographed marks and meter displays) that independent checks can later contradict. Predicted against measured Rx, sheath against drawn length, and OLT export against mapped path are the checks that find problems no form can. That argues for reconciliation as the core feature, with data-entry forms as its feeder. A second implication is that FiberGIS should collect what the literature lacks, because no public study measures phone accuracy in Indian cities, LCO plant structure or the error rates of low-literate mapping, so its own pilot logs would become the best evidence available.

For project skills, this divides naturally into five units with different trust levels: FTTH engineering (the firmest, anchored on G.984.2), HFC engineering (profile-driven and provisional), OSP records and length (mostly reasoned rules), India compliance (dated reference data with legal review), and field UX (hypotheses with test criteria). Each skill should state its evidence grade on each rule and require any new threshold to arrive as editable data with a source and date. A number written into code will outlive the excerpt it came from, and most of the numbers here came from excerpts.
